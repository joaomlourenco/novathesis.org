The [**nova**thesis repository](https://github.com/joaomlourenco/novathesis)
has passed a thousand stars on GitHub. A thousand and one, at the time of
writing.

A star costs nothing and means little on its own. What makes this one worth a
short post is what sits underneath it. The first commit in the repository is
dated **March 2010**, four years before the project arrived on GitHub. In the
sixteen years since, it has grown to **3142 commits** and **49 people** in its
history, and today it carries **67 school configurations across 10
universities** and writes theses in **16 languages** — from Portuguese and
English to Greek, Ukrainian, and both written forms of Chinese.

Those 49 names are the part that matters. Almost every school in the template
is there because someone who had to write their own thesis sat down with their
faculty's formatting rules and turned them into a configuration that everyone
after them could use. Some contributed a full school, some a single fixed
cover, some a bug report precise enough to make the fix obvious. The template
is the accumulation of those.

The **182 forks** tell a quieter story. Some are contributions on their way
back; most are people who took the template somewhere it did not officially
go — a school we do not support, a set of rules we have never seen. That is
what the licence is for, and it is a fine thing to do with it.

## If you want to add to the number

The most useful thing you can do is not a star.

* **Your school is missing?** Open a
  [New school issue](https://github.com/joaomlourenco/novathesis/issues/new?template=request-to-support-a-new-school.md)
  with your faculty's formatting rules and media kit. That is how most of the
  67 got here.
* **A translation reads badly?** Several were done automatically and have
  never been reviewed by a native speaker. Corrections are very welcome.
* **Something is broken?** A report that says which school, which command, and
  what the log said is worth more than a star.

To everyone who has filed an issue, sent a pull request, corrected a
translation, or simply told a colleague that this exists: thank you. The
number is yours more than mine.
