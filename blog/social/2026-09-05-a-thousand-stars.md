### LinkedIn — EN

novathesis passed a thousand stars on GitHub this week — 1,001 when I looked.

It is a vanity number, and on its own it says very little. What it sits on top of is worth more: the first commit in the repository is dated March 2010, four years before the project moved to GitHub. Since then it has grown to 3,142 commits and 49 people in its history, and it now carries 67 school configurations across 10 universities and writes theses in 16 languages — from Portuguese and English to Greek, Ukrainian, and both written forms of Chinese.

Those 49 names are the part worth celebrating. Almost every school in the template is there because a student who had their own dissertation to finish sat down with their faculty's formatting regulations and turned them into a configuration that everyone after them could reuse. Some contributed an entire school, some a corrected cover, some a bug report precise enough to make the fix obvious.

If your institution is not on the list, that is the most useful thing anyone can do with this post: open a "New school" issue with your faculty's formatting rules and media kit, and the template becomes available to every student there. That is how most of the 67 arrived.

To everyone who filed an issue, sent a pull request, fixed a translation, or simply told a colleague that this exists — thank you.

## Reddit — r/LaTeX (title + body; no hashtags — Reddit uses subreddit flair)

**Title:** novathesis passed 1,000 GitHub stars — the number behind it (3142 commits, 49 contributors, 67 school configs)

**Body:**

Small milestone update on the novathesis thesis template, since a chunk of this community has used or contributed to it.

The repo just passed 1,000 stars — 1,001 as of writing. On its own that means little, so here's what's under it: first commit is from March 2010, four years before the project moved to GitHub. Since then: 3142 commits, 49 contributors, 67 school configurations across 10 universities, and theses supported in 16 languages, from Portuguese and English to Greek, Ukrainian, and both written forms of Chinese. 182 forks, most of them people adapting the template to a school or ruleset it doesn't officially support.

If your school is missing, a [New school issue](https://github.com/joaomlourenco/novathesis/issues/new?template=request-to-support-a-new-school.md) with the formatting rules and media kit is the fastest way in — that's how most of the 67 got here.
